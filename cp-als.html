Research project · 2026 – Present

Local Convergence Analysis of CP-ALS for Tensor Completion

University of Notre Dame · Advisor: Prof. Yuefeng Han

I study local convergence of synchronous CP alternating least squares (ALS) for noisy tensor completion under a fixed Bernoulli observation mask.

Inspired by Tang, Chhor, Klopp, and Zhang, Revisit CP Tensor Decomposition: Statistical Optimality and Fast Convergence, this project investigates what changes when only a randomly selected subset of tensor entries is observed.

Numerical results are from separate experiments and do not verify every theorem condition.

1. Background: CP decomposition and partial observation

A canonical polyadic (CP) decomposition represents a tensor as a sum of rank-one outer products. For a \(d\)-way tensor of rank \(R\), consider

\[ \mathcal X=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\lambda_r\,a_{1,r}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{d,r},\qquad \|a_{k,r}\|_2=1. \]

Here, \(a_{k,r}\in\mathbb R^{n_k}\) is the \(r\)-th factor in mode \(k\), and \(\lambda_r\) controls its scale. Unlike tensor decomposition from a fully observed array, tensor completion uses only some entries, with noise:

\[\delta_{\omega}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q),\quad \Omega=\{\omega:\delta_\omega=1\},\quad Y_{\omega}=\mathcal X_{\omega}+Z_{\omega}\quad(\omega\in\Omega).\]

The sampling probability \(q\) is the expected fraction of observed entries. The same mask \(\Omega\) and the same realized noise are used at every iteration. We analyze the Jacobi/synchronous version of ALS: all mode updates in iteration \(t+1\) use only factors from iteration \(t\).

2. The new ingredient: row-wise masked least-squares updates

For a mode \(k\), form the Khatri–Rao design using the current normalized factors from all other modes:

\[ U_k^{(t)}=\bigodot_{j\ne k}\widehat A_j^{(t)}\in\mathbb R^{N_k\times R},\qquad N_k=\prod_{j\ne k} n_j.\]

In the fully observed setting, every mode-\(k\) row shares the same design and hence the same Gram matrix. With missing entries, however, row \(i\) has its own subset of observations. Let \(y_{k,i}\in\mathbb R^{N_k}\) denote that row of the mode-\(k\) unfolding, and let \(D_{k,i}\) be the diagonal matrix indicating its observed entries.

Conceptual illustration, not sampled experimental data. Filled cells are observed; light cells are missing.

The exact row update minimizes a weighted least-squares objective:

\[ b_{k,i}^{(t+1)}=\underset{b\in\mathbb R^R}{\arg\min}\;\frac12\|D_{k,i}(y_{k,i}-U_k^{(t)}b)\|_2^2.\]

Taking the normal equations gives the observation-dependent row Gram matrix and right-hand side:

\[\underbrace{\bigl(U_k^{(t)\top}D_{k,i}U_k^{(t)}\bigr)}_{G_{k,i}^{(t)}}\,b_{k,i}^{(t+1)}=\underbrace{U_k^{(t)\top}D_{k,i}y_{k,i}}_{h_{k,i}^{(t)}}.\]

Equivalently, the Gram matrix is a sum of outer products of the observed design rows:

\[G_{k,i}^{(t)}=\sum_{s:\,(i,s)\in\Omega}u_s^{(t)}u_s^{(t)\top}.\]

Whenever \(G_{k,i}^{(t)}\) is invertible, solve \(b_{k,i}^{(t+1)}=(G_{k,i}^{(t)})^{-1}h_{k,i}^{(t)}\). Stack these solutions into \(B_k^{(t+1)}\), then normalize each factor column. Crucially, the algorithm does not resample, use sample splitting, or add an artificial ridge term.

Sanity check. If \(q=1\), then \(D_{k,i}=I\) for every row, and the update reduces exactly to the familiar fully observed modewise pseudoinverse step \(B_k^{(t+1)}=Y_{(k)}U_k^{(t)}(U_k^{(t)\top}U_k^{(t)})^{-1}\).

3. When are the masked Gram matrices invertible?

The update is only well-defined when \(G_{k,i}^{(t)}\) has full rank. Simply having some observed entries in every row is not enough: the observed design vectors must span \(\mathbb R^R\). At low sampling rates, this can fail and cause numerical instability or a singular least-squares system.

Two structural concepts help explain when conditioning is plausible:

  • Entrywise incoherence bounds the concentration of energy in individual factor coordinates: \(|a_{k,r}[i]|^2\le \mu/n_k\). It prevents the signal from being concentrated on only a few entries.
  • Component coherence measures the overlap between distinct columns: \(\xi=\max_{k,r\ne s}|\langle a_{k,r},a_{k,s}\rangle|\). Small \(\xi\) helps keep the true Khatri–Rao Gram matrix well-conditioned.

Let \(V_k=\bigodot_{j\ne k}A_j\) denote the true Khatri–Rao design. Since the true factors are deterministic in the sampling model, \(\mathbb E[V_k^\top D_{k,i}V_k]=qV_k^\top V_k\). Its diagonal entries equal one; the off-diagonal entries have magnitude at most \(\xi^{d-1}\). Concentration around this expected matrix is a starting point for controlling rowwise conditioning.

Draft result — uniform Gram lower bound (Theorem 3.1 / Lemma 5.1). Under explicit but conservative structural-coherence, sampling, signal-to-noise, and sufficiently accurate warm-start conditions, the manuscript constructs a single high-probability event on which

\[\lambda_{\min}\bigl(G_{k,i}^{(t)}\bigr)\ge\frac{q}{32}\qquad\text{for every iteration }t\ge 0,\text{ mode }k,\text{ and row }i.\]

The conclusion is simultaneous over all updates, not just the first iterate. It is a sufficient condition from an unverified draft, not a claim that the condition is necessary or sharp.

The proof strategy first obtains conditioning for the true masked design and then controls the perturbation caused by an estimated design \(U_k^{(t)}\). The same argument supports uniqueness of every rowwise least-squares solution and makes the update well-defined throughout the proposed local basin.

4. Why keeping one observation mask is difficult

At the first iteration, the design may depend on initialization; at later iterations it certainly depends on earlier least-squares solutions. Since those solutions were computed from the same mask, the current \(U_k^{(t)}\) is statistically dependent on \(D_{k,i}\). In particular, it would be invalid to treat \(U_k^{(t)}\) as a fixed design and casually write \(\mathbb E[U_k^{(t)\top}D_{k,i}U_k^{(t)}]=q\,U_k^{(t)\top}U_k^{(t)}\).

Rather than assuming independence between an iterate and the observations, the current manuscript develops fixed-data high-probability events that remain useful for every iterate in a local neighborhood:

True-factor tangent spaces

Build fixed linear spaces for first-order variations of the true Khatri–Rao columns; use incoherence to control their coordinate leverage.

Simultaneous mask concentration

Apply matrix Bernstein inequalities to projections involving these fixed spaces, across all modes and rows, with a union bound.

Weighted-fiber and noise control

Bound the observed weighted counts along true-factor fibers and the injective norm of the masked noise tensor, uniformly over data-dependent multilinear designs.

Deterministic perturbation argument

Once that single event holds, control the Gram matrices and factor updates for any trajectory remaining in the specified basin, without reusing independence incorrectly.

This is the central conceptual difference between a proof for fully observed ALS and a proposed proof for partially observed ALS with sample reuse.

5. Local convergence: current theoretical progress

The manuscript measures factor error after a common component permutation and modewise sign alignment. Under the same conservative assumptions and a sufficiently small initial error \(\varepsilon_0\), it derives the following quadratic–linear–noise recursion as a working theorem:

\[\boxed{\ \varepsilon_{t+1}\le A\varepsilon_t^2+\bigl(B_{\mathrm{coh}}+B_{\mathrm{samp}}\bigr)\varepsilon_t+\nu\ }\]

The four pieces have distinct meanings:

TermInterpretation
\(A\varepsilon_t^2\)Higher-order terms in the multilinear factor perturbation.
\(B_{\mathrm{coh}}\varepsilon_t\)Residual first-order coupling between nonorthogonal CP components.
\(B_{\mathrm{samp}}\varepsilon_t\)Additional first-order error induced by incomplete observations.
\(\nu\)Additive noise term; determines a neighborhood rather than exact recovery.

Under the proposed assumptions, the recursion yields a local contraction bound

\[\varepsilon_t\le 2^{-t}\varepsilon_0+2\nu\left(1-2^{-t}\right),\qquad\limsup_{t\to\infty}\varepsilon_t\le 2\nu.\]

The draft also recovers the complete-observation limit: when \(q=1\), the sampling-induced linear coefficient vanishes. When noise is zero, the draft bound gives geometric local convergence; a faster quadratic-dominated stage may occur when the linear coefficients are sufficiently small. It does not claim universal quadratic convergence for partial observation.

Interpretation and status. This is a conservative nonasymptotic proof framework in a working manuscript. The argument is intended to hold even when the warm start depends on the observed data, as long as it enters the stated local basin. Independent proof verification and a reduction of the strong sampling requirements remain ongoing tasks.

6. Numerical evidence: rapid recovery and its limits

Separate synthetic experiments evaluate the original synchronous rowwise CP-ALS update across observation rates, component correlation, initialization schemes, and noise levels. The figures illustrate favorable regimes, but they are not a numerical verification of all assumptions in the conservative theorem above.

Independent validation and sensitivity to initialization

At \(\xi=0.2,q=5\%\), 1,000 fresh datasets per initialization family gave 1,000/1,000 successful recoveries for each of two warm starts (median 60 iterations), versus 988/1,000 for Gaussian random starts (median 90). These are separate from the 35 paired experiments.

Sampling rate, coherence, and instability

Increasing the sampling rate can reduce the iterations required in some low-coherence regimes. However, high component correlation produces genuine unstable regions, even for accurate warm starts. An honest interpretation of CP-ALS requires both observations.

Noisy observations

With additive noise, the useful target is a noise-limited reconstruction error, not exact recovery. The experiments measure how quickly the methods enter a retrospectively defined error neighborhood.

7. Limitations and next questions

The draft's main sampling assumption controls weighted observations along one-mode fibers. This is a particularly strong requirement and may be far from the sample sizes at which the algorithm performs well empirically. The manuscript also does not establish a concrete data-only initializer, a global convergence result, minimax-optimal sampling, or sharp constants.

A next step is to replace worst-case bounds on the nonlinear, sample-dependent residual with trajectory-specific concentration estimates—possibly using leave-one-row-out analysis—while retaining control of the smallest eigenvalue of each masked Gram matrix at lower sampling rates.

Research takeaway. Partial observation changes both the algorithmic update and the probabilistic proof. The current work derives the exact rowwise update and develops a conservative route from fixed-data concentration to uniform Gram invertibility and local contraction. The challenging open direction is a sharp guarantee that matches sparse-sampling numerical behavior.

Reference: R. Tang, J. Chhor, O. Klopp, and A. R. Zhang, Revisit CP Tensor Decomposition: Statistical Optimality and Fast Convergence, arXiv:2505.23046. The masked-completion analysis discussed here is separate original research in progress, not a theorem from that reference.
